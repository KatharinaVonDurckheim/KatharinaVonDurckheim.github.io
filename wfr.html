Wildlife Free to Roam

Connecting landscapes for wildlife and people

Research, capacity building and conservation science for maintaining functional ecological connectivity across African landscapes.

Why Wildlife Free to Roam?

Wildlife Free to Roam grew out of my early research on elephant movement and pathways through the Caprivi Strip in Namibia, where I began exploring how elephants navigate increasingly anthropogenic landscapes and what enables them to move between critical resources and protected areas.

This work highlighted the importance of maintaining functional ecological networks — not only protected areas and corridors, but also stepping stones and permeable landscapes — for the long-term persistence of wide-ranging species such as elephants under global change.

Building on this foundation, I joined forces with Stellenbosch University and the Peace Parks Foundation to establish Wildlife Free to Roam as a collaborative research and capacity-building initiative addressing a central conservation question: how can we connect landscapes functionally in ways that allow both people and wildlife to persist and move through shared landscapes?

Purpose

To strengthen the evidence, skills and partnerships needed to understand and conserve wildlife movement across protected areas, international borders and human-modified landscapes.

Our research asks how ecological networks can remain functionally connected: where animals move, which pathways and stepping stones matter, where connectivity is being lost, and how conservation interventions can maintain movement while accommodating the needs of people sharing these landscapes.

Core activities

  • Applied animal movement and spatial ecology research
  • Ecological connectivity and corridor science
  • Human–wildlife coexistence research
  • Postgraduate training and mentoring
  • Quantitative movement-ecology capacity building
  • Partnerships with conservation practitioners
  • Translation of science into management and policy

From movement to connectivity

Wildlife Free to Roam uses animal movement data to move beyond mapping where wildlife occurs towards understanding how landscapes function. By examining movement behaviour, resource selection, barriers, risk and connectivity across scales, we aim to identify the landscape features and ecological processes that enable wide-ranging wildlife to move and persist.

Elephants provide a particularly powerful model for this work because their extensive movements cross ecological, political and human land-use boundaries. The broader objective, however, is to develop approaches that can inform the conservation of connected landscapes for multiple wide-ranging species.

Collaboration

Wildlife Free to Roam works through collaborative research, postgraduate training and partnerships between universities, conservation organisations, protected-area managers and researchers. The initiative is being developed in collaboration with Stellenbosch University and the Peace Parks Foundation, linking academic research with practical conservation challenges across southern Africa.
